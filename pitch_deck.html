Why we built this

Most of us just wait for things to break.

How it works

You don't need new hardware. You need someone who's really listening.

Every experienced technician can hear a failing bearing before they ever check a gauge. GridSense learned to listen the same way, in three simple steps, with nothing to install and nothing to wire up.

Step one

Capture

Record a few seconds of the machine running, or load a sound file. A phone microphone is enough.

Step two

Listen

GridSense breaks the sound into its underlying frequencies and compares it against what healthy and failing machines sound like.

Step three

Act

You get a health score, the likely cause, and the one thing to do next, in plain language.

How we trained it without a lab

We couldn't afford a shelf of failing motors, so we built one in code.

A physics-based audio generator models a healthy motor as a harmonic hum with background noise, friction as an added high-frequency grinding component, and imbalance as a slow low-frequency wobble in loudness, each with randomized severity so the model can't just memorize one sound.

This also means anyone judging this project can regenerate the dataset and retrain the model themselves in a couple of minutes, with `python generate_synthetic_data.py` and `python train_model.py`.

Common questions

What people usually ask when they see this for the first time.

Does it need an internet connection?
No, never. Every computation, from the FFT to the final score, runs locally on your device.
Will background noise confuse it?
The model is trained with realistic background noise mixed in, but a quiet room and 2-3 seconds recorded close to the machine give the most reliable reading.
What if I don't have a failing machine to test on?
The app ships with 240 pre-generated sample clips across all three conditions, so you can try every verdict without a single physical motor.
Can it damage my device or the machine?
No. GridSense only listens. It never controls, connects to, or otherwise interacts with the machine itself.
